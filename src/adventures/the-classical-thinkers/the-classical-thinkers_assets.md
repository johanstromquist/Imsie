# The Classical Thinkers - Asset Requirements

## Overview

**Visual Style:** Classical Greek artistic aesthetic blending ancient pottery styles with Renaissance idealization
- Art style reference: Red-figure and black-figure Greek pottery, Renaissance paintings of antiquity, neoclassical art
- Inspiration: Classical Greek sculpture, Raphael's "School of Athens," ancient Greek architecture, marble and stone textures
- Tone: Contemplative, scholarly, noble, historically evocative without being dry

**Color Palette:**
- Primary: #1a3a52 (Deep blue - philosophy, wisdom, Mediterranean sky)
- Secondary: #d4a574 (Warm gold - ancient Greece, sunlight on marble)
- Accent 1: #8b4513 (Terracotta - pottery, earth, groundedness)
- Accent 2: #e8dcc4 (Aged marble - classical architecture, timelessness)

**Artistic Style Guidelines:**
All images should evoke classical Greek aesthetics with clean lines, balanced compositions, and a sense of timeless wisdom. Use warm Mediterranean tones (terra cotta, ochre, deep blue, olive green), incorporate marble and stone textures, show columned architecture where appropriate. Style should be accessible and engaging for modern students while maintaining historical authenticity. Figures should be noble and contemplative, not stiff or overly formal. Avoid overly dark or gloomy tones—philosophy should feel enlightening, not oppressive.

**Prompt Format:**
All image generation prompts follow this structure:
- **Tone prefix:** "Classical Greek philosophy:"
- **Content description:** [Subject, composition, mood]
- **Style suffix:** "In the style blending ancient Greek pottery aesthetics with Renaissance idealism, warm Mediterranean tones (terracotta, ochre, deep blue, olive), marble and stone textures, columned architecture, educational and contemplative --ar [ratio]"

**Example:**
```
Classical Greek philosophy: Ancient Greek agora at golden hour, marble columns casting long shadows, philosophers in togas engaged in discussion, olive trees, sense of intellectual vitality. In the style blending ancient Greek pottery aesthetics with Renaissance idealism, warm Mediterranean tones (terracotta, ochre, deep blue, olive), marble and stone textures, columned architecture, educational and contemplative --ar 16:9
```

## Gallery Mini-Game Assets (Chapter 4: Plato)

### Overview
The Gallery mini-game demonstrates Plato's Theory of Forms through concrete, recognizable objects. Players see a perfect Form (target) and must click on shadows/instances of that Form while avoiding shadows from other Forms.

**Scene:** Chapter 4, Scene 5 (after reading the Allegory of the Cave)
**Educational Purpose:** Show how multiple imperfect instances participate in one perfect Form
**Game Duration:** 4 rounds, ~2 minutes total

**Key Design Decision:** We only need to create shadows for CORRECT instances. Wrong answers are automatically generated by using shadows from the OTHER Forms as distractors.
