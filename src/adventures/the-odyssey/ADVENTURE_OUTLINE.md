# The Odyssey - Detailed Outline

## Adventure Overview
- **Title:** The Odyssey
- **Estimated Duration:** 4 hours
- **Educational Level:** Grades 8-12
- **Primary Subjects:** Literature, Ancient History, Greek Mythology
- **Visual Style:** Frank Miller's 300 - high contrast, dramatic shadows, bold compositions, burnished bronze/sepia tones

## Learning Objectives
1. Understand the structure and themes of ancient Greek epic poetry
2. Analyze the concept of the hero's journey and its influence on Western literature
3. Explore the role of fate, gods, and human agency in Greek mythology
4. Recognize the historical and cultural context of ancient Greece
5. Examine themes of loyalty, perseverance, and homecoming

## Chapter Breakdown

### Prologue: The Fall of Troy and the Hero's Challenge
**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand the historical context of the Trojan War
- Recognize the epic tradition in Greek literature
- Identify key characters and their relationships
- Explore the role of divine intervention in Greek mythology

**Scene Flow:** (10 scenes)
1. narrative - Opening: Setting the stage after Troy's fall
2. narrative - Introduce Odysseus and his ten-year absence
3. primary-source - Examine ancient Greek map of the Mediterranean
4. narrative - The gods' council on Olympus
5. dialogue - Zeus and Athena debate Odysseus's fate
6. narrative - Penelope's plight in Ithaca
7. decision - Player chooses how Telemachus should respond to suitors
8. narrative - Telemachus's quest for news of his father
9. quote-attribution - Identify famous quotes from The Odyssey
10. narrative - Transition to Odysseus's journey

**Key Themes:** Divine intervention, heroic code, loyalty, honor
**Quiz Topics:** Historical context, epic poetry structure, character motivations

### Chapter 1: The Lotus-Eaters and the Cyclops
**Learning Objectives:**
- Analyze Odysseus's leadership qualities and flaws
- Understand the theme of temptation and self-control
- Explore Greek concepts of hospitality (xenia)
- Recognize hubris as a tragic flaw

**Scene Flow:** (12 scenes)
1. narrative - Odysseus's fleet departs from Troy
2. narrative - Storm drives ships to land of Lotus-Eaters
3. decision - Choose which men to send ashore
4. narrative - The lotus's effect on the crew
5. dialogue - Odysseus confronts his drugged men
6. narrative - Arrival at the Cyclops's island
7. map-exploration - Explore the island and locate Polyphemus's cave
8. narrative - The Cyclops traps the men in his cave
9. decision - Choose Odysseus's strategy to escape
10. narrative - The blinding of Polyphemus
11. dialogue - Odysseus reveals his true name (hubris moment)
12. narrative - Poseidon's curse upon Odysseus

**Key Themes:** Temptation, leadership, hubris, divine retribution, hospitality
**Quiz Topics:** Xenia, hubris, narrative symbolism

### Chapter 2: Circe and the Underworld
**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand the Greek conception of the afterlife
- Analyze transformation as a literary device
- Explore the role of prophecy in Greek literature
- Recognize female figures of power in Greek mythology

**Scene Flow:** (13 scenes)
1. narrative - Journey to Aeaea, Circe's island
2. narrative - Scouts transformed into swine
3. dialogue - Odysseus encounters Hermes, receives moly herb
4. decision - Confront Circe directly or use stealth
5. narrative - Odysseus resists Circe's magic
6. dialogue - Circe becomes an ally
7. narrative - A year passes on Aeaea
8. narrative - Circe directs Odysseus to the Underworld
9. primary-source - Ancient Greek beliefs about the afterlife
10. narrative - Descent into Hades
11. dialogue - Odysseus speaks with the shade of Tiresias
12. anachronism - Identify items that don't belong in ancient Hades
13. narrative - Prophecy of the journey home

**Key Themes:** Transformation, prophecy, death and afterlife, temptation
**Quiz Topics:** Greek underworld, prophecy, character development

### Chapter 3: The Sirens and Scylla & Charybdis
**Learning Objectives:**
- Analyze the theme of knowledge versus safety

- Recognize foreshadowing and dramatic irony

**Scene Flow:** (11 scenes)
1. narrative - Return from the Underworld with knowledge
2. narrative - Circe warns of dangers ahead
3. decision - Choose how to handle the Sirens' song
4. narrative - Odysseus bound to the mast

6. narrative - Successfully passing the Sirens
7. narrative - Approaching the strait of Scylla and Charybdis
8. decision - Choose which danger to face
9. narrative - Loss of six men to Scylla

11. narrative - Grief and the burden of command

**Key Themes:** Impossible choices, sacrifice, knowledge, temptation
**Quiz Topics:** Leadership ethics, mythological creatures, decision consequences

### Chapter 4: The Cattle of Helios and Calypso's Isle
**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand the consequences of breaking divine law

**Scene Flow:** (10 scenes)
1. narrative - Arrival at Thrinacia (Helios's island)

3. decision - Crew debate: starve or eat the sacred cattle
4. narrative - The crew's fatal choice while Odysseus sleeps
5. dialogue - Helios demands vengeance from Zeus
6. narrative - Zeus destroys the ship; only Odysseus survives
7. narrative - Washed ashore on Ogygia, Calypso's island
8. timeline-game - Order the major events of the journey so far
9. narrative - Seven years of captivity with Calypso
10. dialogue - Athena pleads for Odysseus's release

**Key Themes:** Hubris, consequences, loyalty, divine justice, captivity
**Quiz Topics:** Divine law, crew loyalty, chronology of events

**Learning Objectives:**

4. narrative - Odysseus reveals his identity and tells his tale
5. narrative - The Phaeacians provide safe passage to Ithaca
6. dialogue - Athena disguises Odysseus as a beggar
7. narrative - Reunion with Eumaeus the swineherd
8. narrative - Telemachus returns from his quest
9. decision - When should Odysseus reveal his identity?
10. narrative - The beggar in the palace
11. map-exploration - Navigate Odysseus's palace
12. narrative - The contest of the bow
13. dialogue - Odysseus reveals himself to Penelope
14. narrative - The slaughter of the suitors

**Key Themes:** Identity, justice, revenge, loyalty, homecoming
**Quiz Topics:** Recognition scenes, justice vs. revenge, character loyalty

**Learning Objectives:**

3. narrative - Odysseus meets his father Laertes
4. narrative - The families of the suitors seek revenge
5. narrative - Athena brokers peace
6. primary-source - Examine ancient Greek pottery depicting The Odyssey
7. narrative - The oral tradition and Homer
8. timeline-game - Place The Odyssey in historical context
9. narrative - The Odyssey's influence on later works
10. quote-attribution - Match famous literary heroes to their Odyssean traits

**Key Themes:** Legacy, oral tradition, literary influence, peace and reconciliation
**Quiz Topics:** Literary history, epic tradition, thematic influence
